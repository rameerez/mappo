Other worlds

mappo drawing things that are not the Earth. The engine has no idea: it asks a body whether a given latitude and longitude is inside or outside. On Earth that means land against sea, on the Moon maria against highlands, on Mars the northern lowlands against the southern highlands. One question, three spheres.

Opting in

Earth is in the bundle because a world map is what people came for. Everything else is a separate file you ask for by name — the Moon is 11 KB gzipped and Mars is 8 KB, and nobody putting a world map in a hero section should download either. A tag per body is sugar on top: it sets the default, and body= still wins if you write both.

import { registerBody, defineBodyElement } from "mappo";
import { MOON } from "mappo/bodies/moon";
import { MARS } from "mappo/bodies/mars";

registerBody(MOON);
registerBody(MARS);
defineBodyElement("mappo-moon", "moon");
defineBodyElement("mappo-mars", "mars");

<mappo-world body="moon">   <!-- always works -->
<mappo-moon>                 <!-- the same thing, said shorter -->
<mappo-earth>                <!-- ships with mappo; an alias of mappo-world -->

Outlines, not pixels

A body can carry vector outlines as well as a bitmask, and the Moon now does — traced from the same threshold the mask uses, so the line and the squares are the same boundary at two levels of detail. That is exactly the relationship Earth's coastline has with Earth's bitmask. Left is land="outline"; right is the grid it came from.

land-source="vector"

5,859 points, simplified to a fifth of a degree. A mare edge is smooth in nature and only looked like a staircase because we sampled it on a grid.

land-source="grid"

The bitmask the outline was traced from, filled. Same boundary, drawn as cells — and the cells follow cols, so resolution is a slider.

Flat, too

Nothing about the flat renderer is Earth-shaped either. Both of these are the same component in mode="flat", with a different pack behind them.

The Moon · maria

The near side is on the right and left edges; the empty middle is the far side, which is nearly all highland.

Mars · the dichotomy

One clean line around the planet: everything north of it is a third of the surface and several kilometres lower than everything south.

Mars, and where anyone has managed to stop

Nine landings, and almost all of them low, flat and near the equator — the same constraint that kept Apollo near the lunar equator. Teal is a landing region that gets argued about for crewed missions; violet is there for scale.

Three worlds, one renderer, true relative size

Drawn at the same moment by the same code. Mars is half the Earth across and the Moon is a quarter — and Mars has more dry land than the Earth does, which is the sort of thing this picture is for.

Maria from the Clementine 750 nm albedo mosaic; Martian elevation from MOLA (both public domain, NASA/USGS). Each threshold is set to reproduce a published figure — maria at 16% of the lunar sphere, lowlands at a third of Mars — and then checked against places whose heights are known. Both binaries are interpretations, not coastlines: one is a brightness, the other an elevation, and neither is a shore.
