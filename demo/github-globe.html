A rebuild of the globe on github.com/globe, from its own source: the scene constants, the rotation model, the data timeline and the card are the original's, read off the bundle the page ships. Theirs is three.js: ~12,000 dots from a 400×200 land mask on a sphere of radius 25 under a camera at 220, four lights, a halo shader, tubes for the merged pull requests and instanced cylinders for the open ones, with a hover raycast every ten frames. Here the sphere is distribution="uniform" cols="360" distance="8.8" on mappo's own Natural Earth mask, the lights are three CSS gradients, and the lines are mappo/links: a pink arc per merged pull request drawing in, holding and erasing on the original's 600-index-per-second clock, a blue spike per open one rising and sinking as the data window passes.

The opening view depends on your clock, as theirs does: the globe starts turned by your timezone offset. Drag to spin — the pointer feeds the original's eased velocity, not the angle — and drag up or down to tilt within its limits. Hover a line for its pull request; the globe slows to a stop while the card shows, and a line stays until you let it go. The flag at Svalbard is the Arctic Code Vault. The data is a snapshot of the feed the page reads (webgl-globe/data/data.json, with its fallback.json), so what you see opened and merged is what it showed. More demos.
