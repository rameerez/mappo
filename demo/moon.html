The Moon

mappo drawing something that is not the Earth. The engine has no idea: it asks a body whether a given latitude and longitude is inside or outside, and on the Moon that question means maria against highlands rather than land against sea.

A prototype · mappo · more demos

Opting in

Earth ships in the bundle because a world map is what people came for. Every other body is a separate file you ask for by name — the Moon is 4.5 KB gzipped, and nobody putting a world map in a hero section should download it.

import { registerBody } from "mappo";
import { MOON } from "mappo/bodies/moon";

registerBody(MOON);

<mappo-world body="moon"></mappo-world>

Near side · far side

The side we see

Tidally locked, so this face has looked at us for four billion years. The dark plains are basalt that flooded the big impact basins.

The side we don't

Almost no maria at all — the crust is thicker here, and the lava never got out. Nobody saw this until Luna 3 in 1959.

Where we have been, and where we are going

Six Apollo landings, all of them on the near side and near the equator, because that is where the fuel budget reached. The teal marks are candidate regions for Artemis III — all of them at the south pole, chosen for standing sunlight beside craters whose floors have not seen the Sun in two billion years.

Same code, different sphere

Both drawn by the same renderer at the same moment, at their true relative size — the Moon is a quarter of the Earth across, which is a bigger fraction than any other moon in the solar system manages against its planet.

Maria from the Clementine 750 nm albedo mosaic (public domain, NASA/USGS), thresholded so they cover 16% of the sphere — the published figure. The near side then lands at 30% against a published ~31%, which nothing was tuned for. Mare boundaries are gradational, so this edge is a brightness level and not a shore.
